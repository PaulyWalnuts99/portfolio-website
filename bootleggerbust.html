Overview

Bootlegger Bust is a level set in a Prohibition-era warehouse, designed to support the time period through environmental layout and visual structure. The goal was to create a readable warehouse space with clearly defined areas that guide player movement and support gameplay.

  • Design a warehouse that reflected a Prohibition-era industrial setting while remaining readable for gameplay
  • Organize distinct areas within the warehouse to help players understand space, progression, and purpose at a glance
  • Support gameplay by ensuring room sizes and sightlines were consistent and supported player flow

Level Walkthrough

  • Goal: Introduce the level and player objective through a focused garage space that establishes tone and intent
  • Design action 1: Use minimal, moody lighting and a compact layout to guide player attention toward objectives and on-screen widgets
  • Design action 2: Design the garage as a tight but purposeful space that sets expectations for more complex areas later in the level

Beat 1: Starting Garage

  • Goal: Establish context and immersion with the city street outside the warehouse
  • Design action 1: Introduce framing techniques to guide the player visually toward the warehouse entrance
  • Design action 2: Use surrounding streets and props to intuitively direct player movement toward the objective

Beat 2: City Streets

  • Goal: Introduce the first combat area and support player engagement
  • Design action 1: Provide multiple cover points and clear sightlines for combat encounters
  • Design action 2: Use set dressing and props to create an authentic industrial environment without blocking navigation

Beat 3: Warehouse Part 1

  • Goal: Expand the combat space while establishing a distinct tone and atmosphere
  • Design action 1: Use warm yellow-orange lighting and moody tones to create a vintage, immersive feel
  • Design action 2: Incorporate set dressing and props to convey a Prohibition-era hideout while maintaining clear readability

Beat 4: Warehouse Part 2

  • Goal: Create a focused one-on-one encounter that contrasts the larger combat spaces
  • Design action 1: Use red lighting and dramatic props to heighten tension and reinforce a confrontational tone
  • Design action 2: Provide a brief slowdown connected to the back storage area to let the player reset before the final stretch

Beat 5: Warehouse Office

  • Goal: Introduce a tighter, darker, and more linear space to shift pacing
  • Design action 1: Use minimal lighting and dense shelving to create tension and anticipation
  • Design action 2: Apply breadcrumbing through lighting to intuitively guide player movement

Beat 6: Storage Room

  • Goal: Deliver the final showdown and culminate the level's escalation in difficulty and atmosphere
  • Design action 1: Incorporate a boss health bar to make this encounter feel unique and give the player clear combat feedback
  • Design action 2: Include a key drop mechanic to unlock the door outside the room, linking the encounter to level progression

Beat 7: Final Boss Room

The Process

Research & References

  • Research & Ideation: Referenced Prohibition-era photos and similar game levels to understand how to convey setting, flow, and combat spaces effectively
  • Reference Gathering: Collected images of warehouses, streets, props, lighting setups, and cinematic inspiration to inform environmental design
  • Mapping & Organization: Mapped out room layouts, points of interest, and encounter flow to plan the overall player journey

Initial Blockout

The initial blockouts were designed to establish the warehouse layout and support player engagement throughout the level.

  • Supported Player Mechanics: Created blockouts that provided spaces for combat, cover usage, and navigation, reinforcing core gameplay flow within the warehouse
  • Visual and Tactical Clarity: Arranged key architectural features, doors, and props to clearly communicate paths, cover, and engagement areas
  • Contextualized Playspaces: Designed each room and corridor to feel intentional and connected to Prohibition-era reference imagery

Design Challenges

Problem 1: Unclear Critical Path

  • Goal: Create a warehouse environment that guides the player naturally through exploration and combat
  • Problem: Early layouts made it unclear where players were supposed to go, and the critical path was tedious to follow
  • Solution: Ditched ladders and unclear doors, replacing them with a more linear and intuitive path design using clear visual cues like stairs

Unclear progression in first room

Original level — only three combat beats

Problem 2: Level Too Short With Little Variance

  • Goal: Create a level that provided 5–8 minutes of engaging gameplay with varied beats
  • Problem: The original layout was far too short, consisting of only three brief gameplay beats
  • Solution: Added a starting garage and city for slower contextual buildup, plus a back storage room and final boss to increase intensity and differentiate gameplay beats

Conclusion & Learnings

Designing Bootlegger Bust provided a focused opportunity to refine level flow, player guidance, and gameplay pacing within a visually cohesive Prohibition-era setting.

Knowledge Gained

  • Learned how to structure spaces and visual cues to naturally guide players through complex layouts
  • Gained insight into differentiating combat and exploration beats to maintain engagement and tension
  • Developed skills in framing key objectives and designing spaces that clearly communicate player intent

Areas for Growth

  • Improve early-stage layout planning to better anticipate pacing and player flow
  • Refine environmental storytelling without relying on explicit narrative cues
  • Experiment with more dynamic encounter design to create varied combat experiences
