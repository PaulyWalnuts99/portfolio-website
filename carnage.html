Project Overview

Carnage is a fast-paced mobile game inspired by Burnout 3: Takedown's iconic crash mode, where players navigate dense traffic zones to chain collisions and score points. As a level designer, I:

  • Designed and iterated city layouts to create engaging crash scenarios that balanced chaos with player control
  • Implemented procedural placement of obstacles, hazards, and interactive objects to ensure each run felt dynamic and unpredictable

Goals & Constraints

Project Goals

  • Develop multiple city layouts with distinct visual and structural identities for unique gameplay experiences
  • Introduce variability through modular segments and dynamic object placement to keep each playthrough unpredictable
  • Balance procedural placement and object density for mobile performance while keeping levels readable and interactive

Production Constraints

  • Levels had to be designed, tested, and refined within a 10-week production window, requiring efficient layout decisions
  • Object density and procedural segments had to maintain performance while keeping levels engaging
  • Randomly placed hazards had to create fun and fair gameplay while preserving level structure

Concept & Inspiration

The concept originated from my pitch to the team, inspired by my long-time love for Burnout 3: Takedown. While many student projects focus on platformers or small-scope ideas, I wanted something different — a high-energy driving game that brought large-scale chaos to mobile. A cel-shaded art style gave the project a distinctive modern look while maintaining performance efficiency for mobile hardware.

Procedural Generation Implementation

Blockouts & Grayboxing

In the grayboxing phase, I created the initial blockout for our first level, San Francisco. Once the base layout was established, the other level designers iterated on it — rearranging buildings, modifying layouts, and creating new prefabs. This produced over 10 unique variants of San Francisco, with virtually infinite possibilities thanks to our procedural generation system. I personally created the original blockout plus five additional variants.

Iteration Phase

During iteration, the team focused on bringing graybox levels to life with art assets and set dressing. Key systems were added — UI components, audio, fully modeled city parts, the player's car, all powerups, and procedural generation integration. This phase was crucial for testing how all systems worked together in a visually complete environment.

Final Renderings

All core systems and features are fully implemented. The game now includes two fully playable tracks — San Francisco and Las Vegas — each featuring iconic landmarks: the Transamerica Pyramid and the Sphere. Added unique collision sounds, VFX for powerup pickups, and a car shop where players can purchase custom paint jobs with Crash Dollars.

Conclusion & Learnings

Over the course of this ten-week project, our team made significant progress creating a playable and engaging mobile driving experience. Considering our limited experience with procedural generation and mobile optimization, we were proud of what we accomplished in such a short time.

Key Learnings

  • Creating grayboxes and blockouts early allowed rapid iteration and testing of mechanics
  • Collaborating effectively with multiple level designers to create variants and procedural systems
  • Documenting design decisions to maintain clear team communication
  • Managing scope and timeline in a 10-week project while balancing team obligations

Areas for Growth

  • Deeper early planning for procedural systems to reduce iteration time
  • More rigorous mobile performance testing throughout development
  • Earlier integration of art assets to better visualize level readability
