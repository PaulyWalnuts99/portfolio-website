Level Overview

Overview

S.O.R.N is a fast-paced, third-person mech shooter built around large-scale combat and dynamic traversal. In Mission 1, the player is deployed into a vast desert to neutralize an enemy compound guarding a monolith-scale transportation facility known as The Wall — operated by a hostile PMC. After clearing the desert, the player infiltrates the facility sector by sector, plants a bomb in the control room, and extracts before time runs out. The desert serves as a proving ground for core mechanics, while The Wall escalates the challenge through tighter spaces, environmental hazards, and increasingly complex encounters.

Level Goals

  • Teach core mech combat and traversal mechanics through escalating encounter design across the desert and facility
  • Guide the player naturally from the open desert into the tight, high-stakes interior of The Wall without hand-holding
  • Build tension and pacing across 5 distinct beats — from exploration and learning to infiltration and extraction

Beat Breakdown

Beat 01

The Desert

Design Intent
  • Goal: Introduce players to core combat mechanics through a focused enemy compound encounter, using the environment to naturally direct movement and reinforce spatial awareness
  • Design Action 1: Positioned the enemy compound directly at the base of The Wall, using flanking rock formations to funnel the player in and eliminate navigation ambiguity
  • Design Action 2: Mounted a high-visibility laser turret above the compound entrance to serve as a threat landmark, drawing the player's eye to the objective and forcing a deliberate cover-based approach
Beat Video

Playthrough — The Desert

Initial Blockout & Flaws
  • The Space: An enemy compound at the base of The Wall guarding the facility entrance — the player must clear it and locate the maintenance entry point to infiltrate
  • Flaw 1: The player had no line of sight on any objective — without a visible landmark or threat to react to, there was no logical decision point to drive movement or establish a sense of direction
  • Flaw 2: The compound had no defined encounter structure — enemy positions, cover opportunities, and player routing were unestablished, leaving the space with no readable combat flow

Blockout 1 — Undefined critical path

Blockout 2 — Road introduced but compound still lacks identity

Iteration & Solutions
  • Solution 1: Introduced a central road leading from The Wall into the desert with geometry flanking both sides, establishing an unmistakable critical path while preserving cover opportunities
  • Solution 2: Designed the road to fork naturally at the compound — with dialogue confirming the front entrance is locked and a waypoint directing the player, the fork becomes a meaningful decision point rather than a source of confusion

These changes worked with the game's existing systems rather than against them. The road geometry creates a clear decision point, dialogue provides narrative justification, and the waypoint handles explicit direction — the level design supports all three without redundancy

Beat 02

The Wall — Sector 1

Design Intent
  • Goal: Introduce players to interior combat for the first time, establishing the scale of The Wall facility and presenting new enemy types in a controlled, readable encounter space
  • Design Action 1: Kept enemy density low and maneuvering space generous in the first interior encounter — the exit door opens only after enemies are defeated, giving the player a clear objective and a natural reason to engage rather than avoid
  • Design Action 2: Designed Sector 1 as a functioning crystal loading bay — cargo containers and structural columns create natural cover positions while the space communicates The Wall's purpose as an industrial facility, grounding the encounter in the world's narrative
Beat Video

Playthrough — Sector 1

Initial Blockout & Flaws
  • The Space: The player's first interior encounter — a crystal loading bay inside The Wall where cargo is staged and loaded onto trains. The space is designed to introduce interior combat at a manageable difficulty while establishing the facility's industrial scale and purpose
  • Flaw 1: Traversal paths and encounter flow were unclear — playtesters struggled to identify the forward route and frequently backtracked through the space
  • Flaw 2: The exit was positioned on the opposite wall from the entry point with trains obstructing the sightline — players had no readable forward direction and couldn't identify where to progress after clearing the encounter

Blockout 1 — vertical concept scrapped after playtesting feedback

Blockout 2 — sightlines blocked by train geometry

Blockout 3 — central trains and rooms entrance point obscured the exit and enemies

Blockout 4 — overhead view of blind sections

Iteration & Solutions
  • Solution 1: Removed mandatory verticality and platforming from active combat zones — redesigned the sector for linear flow with a clear forward route that guides the player naturally through each encounter
  • Solution 2: Relocated the room entrance to give players an immediate sightline into the encounter space — players now enter with enemies and the exit visible from the threshold, eliminating the blind entry problem

Removing verticality and relocating the entrance resolved both core issues in one pass. The space became immediately readable on entry — players could see enemies, cover, and the exit without the encounter fighting against the environment

Beat 03

The Wall — Sector 2

Design Intent
  • Goal: Design a multi-zone sector built around an active subway network — alternating between combat rooms and train tunnels to create rhythm, disparity, and escalating tension across the space
  • Design Action 1: Structured the sector as three combat rooms separated by three train tunnels — players must navigate moving trains between each encounter, creating a distinct pacing shift between combat and traversal
  • Design Action 2: Used the train tunnels as active hazards rather than passive transitions — timing the player's movement through the tunnels adds mechanical pressure and reinforces spatial awareness before each new encounter
Beat Video

Playthrough — Sector 2

Initial Blockout & Flaws
  • The Space: An underground subway network inside The Wall — three combat rooms alternating with three active train tunnels, designed to create rhythmic disparity between intense encounters and high-pressure traversal
  • Flaw 1: All rooms and tunnels read as identical spaces — without visual differentiation between zones the sector lost its sense of progression and players struggled to gauge where they were in the sequence
  • Flaw 2: Lighting failed to compensate for the lack of spatial variation — with no geometry or visual landmarks to anchor the player, there was no readable way to communicate direction or progression through the sector

Blockout 1 — vertical concept scrapped to to bland design and oversized space

Blockout 2 — no spatial differentiation between zones

Blockout 3 — unlit, undifferentiated corridor

Blockout 4 — identical branching paths with no visual hierarchy

Iteration & Solutions
  • Solution 1: Rebuilt the sector from scratch — new geometry gave each room, hallway, and train tunnel a distinct visual identity, eliminating the repetition that made the space feel directionless
  • Solution 2: Relit the space with intentional contrast — rooms and hallways were well lit while train tunnels remained darker, creating a pull effect that naturally guided players toward the next zone without explicit direction

Starting over on the geometry solved both problems simultaneously. The spatial variation gave players a readable sense of progression, and the lighting contrast did the wayfinding work that the original blockout couldn't achieve

Beat 04

The Wall — Sector 3

Design Intent
  • Goal: Shift pacing with a tighter, more linear section that demands mastery of all mechanics learned in previous beats
  • Design Action 1: Used darker lighting, broken scaffolding, and environmental debris to build atmosphere and create a sense of danger while keeping traversal paths readable
  • Design Action 2: Placed enemies densely in constricted corridors to demand precise movement and punish passive play, marking the escalation toward the final encounter
Beat Video

Playthrough — Sector 3

Initial Blockout & Flaws
  • The Space: A tight, linear corridor section deep inside The Wall — the player must fight through dense enemy placement and navigate a moving train hazard to reach the control room
  • Flaw 1: After defeating the turret guarding the path forward, the drop-down route was not visible — players stalled at a dead end with no clear indication of where to go next
  • Flaw 2: The oncoming train hazard was not telegraphed — players had no readable warning before being hit, making the hazard feel arbitrary rather than skill-based

Blockout 1 — unlit drop-down hole, path forward invisible to players

Blockout 2 — train avoidance space present but visually unreadable, causing confusion

Iteration & Solutions
  • Solution 1: Made the drop-down path clearly visible post-turret using environmental framing — the player's eye is now naturally drawn to the exit without interrupting flow
  • Solution 2: Added two visible safe havens on either side of the train tunnel — players can now confidently time their traversal with clearly readable spaces to retreat to

Both solutions addressed the same root problem — players couldn't read the space. Lighting made the path forward obvious and the safe havens gave players the confidence to progress through the train hazard without hesitation

Targeted lighting guiding the player toward the drop-down critical path

Visible safe havens on both sides enabling confident traversal through the train tunnel

Beat 05

The Final Boss Room

Design Intent
  • Goal: Deliver a climactic boss encounter followed by an escape sequence that reinforces the spatial connectivity of The Wall — bringing the player back through spaces they recognize to exit through the door that was locked at the start
  • Design Action 1: Designed the boss arena to give the player enough space to maneuver freely while keeping the encounter readable and the objective clear
  • Design Action 2: Routed the escape through a chute back into Sector 1 and out the original locked front entrance — rewarding the player with spatial recognition and a sense of closure that ties the level together

Research & References

The process began with research and reference gathering to define the visual and gameplay language of the level before any blockout work started.

  • Studied mech-focused games like Armored Core VI to analyze combat pacing, encounter design, and level flow
  • Collected visual references — concept art, gameplay screenshots, and real-world military/industrial imagery
  • Organized everything on a Miro board to map out layouts, level goals, and design solutions visually before touching the engine

Conclusion & Learnings

Developing S.O.R.N's first level provided a hands-on opportunity to create large-scale 3D environments that balance exploration, combat, and player guidance. Iteration and refinement were key to ensuring pacing, flow, and engagement remained compelling throughout each encounter.

Knowledge Gained

  • Gained experience blocking out levels and using Unreal Engine tools like CubeGrid, landscaping, and foliage
  • Designing with purpose — every prop, encounter, and space reinforces gameplay or narrative
  • Communicating level design concepts clearly through maps, diagrams, and reference visuals

Areas for Growth

  • Exploring more creative layouts and prop placement to enhance player experience
  • Applying feedback effectively while maintaining a cohesive level design
  • Balancing challenge and guidance to better teach player systems through level design
